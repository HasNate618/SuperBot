# Research: Whole-body control architecture for driving a Unitree G1's upper body from external task-space pose targets under balance

## Summary

Every credible system that tracks external head/hand poses on a humanoid *retrains or fine-tunes a single whole-body policy* whose command space includes upper-body pose; the locomotion policy and arm controller are not bolted together at run time. The failure mode is documented on this exact robot: Unitree's own `rt/arm_sdk` arm control runs on top of a balance controller that is unaware of the arms, and integrators report forward-lean, torso oscillation and an infinite stepping loop when arms are held out ([Robotics Knowledgebase — Unitree G1](https://roboticsknowledgebase.com/wiki/common-platforms/unitree-g1/), [unitree_sdk2_python #173](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2_python/issues/173)). For this VR application: build the layered version now (rate-limited joint-space IK for arms + conservative waist yaw for head-look + untouched leg actions + velocity-command bias), with a firm plan to move to a retrained arm-conditioned whole-body policy.

## Findings

**1. SOTA for upper-body tracking under balance.** *(a) Model-based WBC:* prioritized/hierarchical QP with constraints (feet, CoM) top-priority and operational tasks (both hands) next is the classical answer ([Sentis & Khatib, ICRA 2006](https://khatib.stanford.edu/publications/pdfs/Sentis_2006_ICRA.pdf)). Cost is a non-issue: OSF-based HQP solves 5 priority levels on a 33-DoF humanoid (12 arm + 3 waist + 12 leg DoF) in **0.42 ms at 1 kHz** ([DLR IROS 2021](https://elib.dlr.de/147149/2/iros21_204_paper.pdf)); WBDC computes 28-DoF torques in 0.6 ms ([Kim, IROS 2018](https://dhkim0821.github.io/papers/2018_IROS_WBDC.pdf)). That DLR stack — CoM/pelvis orientation → both hands → upper-body orientation → damping — is nearly the hierarchy needed here. *(b) Learned whole-body controllers* replace explicit WBC (below). *(c) Admittance+impedance on the upper body with position control on the legs* is a third route ([Dancing with REEM-C](https://exa.ai/library/publication/1th8yq4xq9r); [GentleHumanoid](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04679) does this inside a G1 motion-tracking policy).

**2. THE KEY CONFLICT.** Four patterns, increasing effort:

- **Intervention training / action override (target).** HUGWBC samples *alternative actions to replace the upper-body actions produced by the whole-body policy during training*, flipping a binary intervention flag with p=0.005, making the policy robust to any external upper-body signal — and reports this also *improves* locomotion tracking ([HUGWBC](https://hugwbc.github.io/resources/HugWBC.pdf)). HOVER does it at the command level via mode + sparsity masks on upper/lower body independently, distilled from an oracle motion imitator by DAgger, yielding >15 modes including "upper body kinematic tracking + lower body velocity tracking" in one policy ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21229), [site](https://hover-versatile-humanoid.github.io/), [NVlabs/HOVER](https://github.com/NVlabs/HOVER/) — default specialist mode is OmniH2O 3-point head+hands).
- **Retrain with an upper-body command space.** ExBody/ExBody2 deliberately do not track lower-body reference motion: arms imitate 3D keypoints, legs follow a root-velocity command, with keypoint and velocity tracking decoupled ([ExBody](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16796), [ExBody2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13196), [code](https://github.com/edpsw/exbody2)). HOMIE adds an *upper-body pose curriculum* so the G1 walks and squats under arbitrary upper-body poses, MoCap-free ([HOMIE](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13013), [code](https://github.com/InternRobotics/OpenHomie)). Cheapest realistic retrain on a Playground/MJX+brax base.
- **Decoupled, with a lever arm for the arms.** HumanPlus's low-level policy emits 19-D body setpoints at 50 Hz under 1000 Hz PD, and passes hand joint targets *directly to the PD controller*, bypassing the policy ([HumanPlus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10454v1)). HOMIE's exoskeleton sets arm joints directly, dispensing with IK. Unitree's `rt/arm_sdk` is this pattern and is the documented failure: the G1 locomotion policy "is not aware of how the arms are being controlled… the policy will attempt to balance the robot by stepping forward" ([Knowledgebase](https://roboticsknowledgebase.com/wiki/common-platforms/unitree-g1/); in Running Mode arm control and velocity commands are mutually exclusive, in Regular Mode both are allowed). See [#146](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2_python/issues/146), [#108](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2_python/issues/108).
- **Residual RL on a frozen base.** ASAP learns a delta/residual action model for sim-to-real mismatch then fine-tunes through it ([ASAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01143v1)); FAST adds a lightweight residual delta policy on a frozen controller under Parseval+KL constraints, `a_t = a_t^b + a_t^r` ([FAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11929)); RuN ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20696v1)) and RobotDancing ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20717v2)) likewise. **Most relevant warning:** RobotDancing's *selective residualization* keeps residual authority on hip/knee-pitch only, excludes ankle pitch, and notes that "torso/arm residuals may reduce local joint error but can reduce global robustness." Don't ask residuals to fix 14 arm joints. Adversarial upper-vs-lower policy training (ALMI) is a variant ([ALMI](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14305v2)).

**3. Head-look with no neck.** The G1, like the Unitree H1, has no neck — H1's only torso DOF is a Z-axis joint and the head is rigid on `torso_link` ([h1.urdf tree](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_ros/blob/df1a4bc9/robots/h1_description/README.md)). OmniH2O nonetheless tracks a head keypoint on H1 from 3-point VR input, and its ablation shows 3-point ≈ 22-point tracking ([OmniH2O](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08858)). So the head pose is absorbed by **waist yaw plus the floating base**. Use a yaw-substitution policy: in HRP-2 teleoperation head yaw beyond **40° relative to the waist** triggered stepping and head yaw directly set future foot yaw ([Steering a Humanoid Robot by Its Head](https://vigir.missouri.edu/~gdesouza/Research/Conference_CDs/IEEE_IROS_2009/papers/0305.pdf)). Gaze allocation across redundant spine/neck/eye joints with a joint-effort cost yields human-like "small joints lead, big joints follow" ([Controlling the gaze direction of a humanoid robot with redundant joints](https://exa.ai/library/publication/2dnmxrvdgbw)). TWIST2 is the counter-example: it *adds* a $250 2-DoF neck, computing yaw/pitch in closed form from a rotation matrix ([TWIST2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02832)).

**4. MuJoCo tooling.** **mink** — differential IK on MuJoCo; `FrameTask`/`RelativeFrameTask`/`ComTask`, joint position+velocity limits, collision avoidance, closed-chain constraints, `qpsolvers` backends; `mujoco>=3.8.1`, `qpsolvers>=4.12` ([github](https://github.com/kevinzakka/mink), [docs](https://kevinzakka.github.io/mink/)). **pink (pin-pink)** — Pinocchio + `qpsolvers`; `FrameTask`, `PostureTask`, `DampingTask`, `ComTask`, `Barriers` (`SelfCollisionBarrier`, `PositionBarrier`); ships a free-flyer humanoid example with CoM, torso-orientation and fixed-foot frame tasks at 200 Hz ([github](https://github.com/stephane-caron/pink/), [JVRC example](https://github.com/stephane-caron/pink/blob/main/examples/humanoid_jvrc.py), [docs](https://stephane-caron.github.io/pink/inverse-kinematics.html)). Pink/Pinocchio wheels are Linux x86_64 only ([Isaac Sim PINK](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/6.1.0/pink/index.html)). **Raw MuJoCo** has no IK solver: `mj_jacSite`/`mj_jacBody` + damped least squares, then `mj_integratePos` → `data.ctrl` ([mjctrl/diffik.py](https://github.com/kevinzakka/mjctrl/blob/main/diffik.py)). **Assets/sim:** Menagerie `unitree_g1` (29 DoF, BSD-3-Clause; `g1.xml`, `scene.xml`, `scene_mjx.xml`, `stand`/`home`/`knees_bent` keyframes; "position actuators (needs tuning)"; MJX variant "successfully transferred to hardware in MuJoCo Playground") ([mujoco_menagerie](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_menagerie/tree/main/unitree_g1)); `unitree_mujoco` is the SDK2-DDS bridge exposing `rt/lowcmd`, `rt/lowstate`, `rt/secondary_imu` (torso IMU, G1 only) ([unitree_mujoco](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_mujoco)). Playground deployment references: [humanoid-locomotion-sim2sim](https://github.com/earosenfeld/humanoid-locomotion-sim2sim) (12-DOF leg policy with *arm joints held at the training default by position actuators*; documents that Isaac Lab's implicit PD at 5 ms is unstable for an explicit PD and must be replaced by a separate ~1 kHz loop), [g1_deploy_mujoco](https://github.com/RoboCubPilot/g1_deploy_mujoco), [G1_deploy_python](https://github.com/TheoBounac/G1_deploy_python).

**5. Rates and latency.** Dominant pattern is **policy 50 Hz / PD 500–1000 Hz**. TeleGate: policy 50 Hz, PD 500 Hz, 37.5 Hz low-pass filter between them, on a **29-DoF G1** in MuJoCo ([TeleGate](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09628v1)). ExtremControl: PD 1000 Hz / policy 50 Hz, velocity-feedforward ratio ≤0.95 bound derived from Δt=0.02 s, and reports that position-only PD alone costs ~100 ms of the end-to-end budget ([ExtremControl](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11321v3)). HumanPlus: 50 Hz policy, 1000 Hz PD, 25 Hz high-level async. HUGWBC deploys its actor at 50 Hz. **Latency:** most humanoid teleop systems sit near **200 ms**; ExtremControl reaches ~50 ms and HuMiT reports ~20 ms comm-to-inference / 80 ms median externally measured, attributing the 200 ms+ norm to future-frame buffering (SONIC: 10 frames × 20 ms) and per-step online IK (TWIST2: >100 ms/frame) ([HuMiT](https://humit-ral.github.io/humit.pdf)). Retargeting is cheap if done right: GMR ≈ 30 ms with IK update limited to ~30 Hz, versus a closed-form Cartesian VR→link mapping at 0.3 ms ([ExtremControl](https://extremcontrol.github.io/)). **At 20–90 Hz targets / 500 Hz physics:** never run IK in the 500 Hz loop — solve on the target tick, hold the joint target, let PD interpolate. Add velocity feedforward from successive targets (η∈{0.8,0.9}; η=1.0 overshoots), low-pass the command, rate-limit joint targets (Unitree's `xr_teleoperate` clips arm targets by velocity before publishing), and predict stale targets rather than stepping them — the Kinematics Streaming Toolbox gets 1 kHz control bandwidth from 60 Hz references via filtering/estimation/prediction, ~70 ms input-to-pose on Nadia ([High-Speed Teleoperation](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04639v1)).

## Recommended architecture

1. **Own the arms kinematically.** mink or pink: two 6-DoF `FrameTask`s on wrist frames + `PostureTask` toward the policy's default arm pose + joint limit/velocity constraints + self-collision barriers. Null space (7 arm DoF for a 6-DoF pose) is resolved by the posture task, not a hand-tuned elbow heuristic.
2. **Never hand the arms an unbounded pose.** Rate-limit, clamp to a balance-safe envelope measured once in MuJoCo, add wrist admittance so contact yields rather than dragging the torso.
3. **Waist = virtual neck.** Yaw split: waist-yaw takes a bounded share (≈±35–40°, the empirically-motivated stepping threshold), remainder becomes a base-yaw steering term in the velocity command. Roll/pitch small, heavily regularized, never free. Waist yaw outranks arm reach; degrade hand targets, not balance.
4. **Legs stay on the pretrained policy.** Keep all 12 leg actions untouched; correct drift/CoM error by biasing the 2D command, not the leg actions. Add leg residuals only if needed — RobotDancing's mask heuristic shows residual authority on balance joints is a stability heuristic, not an accuracy optimum.
5. **Acknowledge the measured limitation:** 0.475 m drift in 5 s at zero command means the policy cannot station-keep; static whole-body reaching will look like shuffling. Accept it, add hip/knee-pitch-only station-keeping residuals, or retrain.
6. **Retrain as endgame, cheaply.** Extend `G1JoystickFlatTerrain` with an upper-body command block (arm joint angles + head yaw + hand keypoints), train with HOMIE-style upper-body pose curriculum and HUGWBC-style intervention training, using HOVER's masking scheme. OmniH2O's 3-point ablation is the evidence that head+hands alone is a sufficient interface.

## Concrete integration notes (severity)

- **HIGH — action scale is not portable.** `unitree_rl_gym/legged_gym/envs/g1/g1_config.py` uses `action_scale = 0.25` for the 12-DoF G1 (`ctrl = action*0.25 + default_angle`), while the Playground G1 policy metadata gives 0.5. Read it from the exported manifest; a wrong scale halves or doubles every leg action ([g1_config.py](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_rl_gym/blob/main/legged_gym/envs/g1/g1_config.py), [issue #95](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_rl_gym/issues/95)).
- **HIGH — waist ownership in `rt/arm_sdk` is ambiguous.** `unitree_sdk2_python/example/g1/high_level/g1_arm5_sdk_dds_example.py` lists `WaistYaw/WaistRoll/WaistPitch` in the arm_sdk joint set, while `xr_teleoperate` defines `G1_29_JointArmIndex` as arms only (15–28). Decide and gate explicitly ([#146](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2_python/issues/146)).
- **MEDIUM —** G1 has PR vs AB ankle control modes and a mechanical ankle-linkage sim-to-real gap; ASAP trained a 4-DoF *ankle-only* delta model for the G1 for exactly this reason ([ASAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01143v1), [G1 SDK binding](https://github.com/EGalahad/unitree_sdk2)).
- **MEDIUM —** Menagerie `unitree_g1/g1.xml` ships "joint position actuators (needs tuning)"; use `scene_mjx.xml` gains for Playground-consistent behavior ([mujoco_menagerie](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_menagerie/tree/main/unitree_g1)).
- **LOW —** `unitree_g1/g1_with_hands.xml` `stand` keyframe places both thumb-1 joints/ctrls 0.0028 rad outside declared limits ([issue #296](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_menagerie/issues/296)).

## Uncertainty flags

- **`HRL` / "Hybrid Robotic Transformer": not verified.** No humanoid paper under that name was found. Nearest verifiable: Figure **Helix** (System 2 VLM at 7–9 Hz; System 1 transformer outputting 35-DoF full-upper-body control at 200 Hz including torso and head orientation targets, with documented head/torso feedback-loop instability) and NVIDIA **HOVER/GR00T** ([Helix](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix), [HOVER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21229)). Do not cite "HRL".
- **G1 waist joint limits not verified.** The ±40° yaw threshold is from HRP-2, not G1 specs. Measure the actual `waist_yaw/pitch/roll` ranges in the loaded MJCF first.
- **Playground policy internals taken as given.** The 103-dim obs layout, 0.5 action scale and ctrl plumbing come from the task context and were not verified against upstream `play_g1_joystick.py`; the Playground paper only confirms joystick actions are "absolute joint position with a default offset" ([MuJoCo Playground](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08844v1)).
- **No source describes this exact combination** — a frozen Playground joystick policy driven with independent arm actions in MuJoCo. It is assembled here from adjacent evidence and should be validated empirically before being treated as known-good.
- **Head-orientation fidelity is structurally capped:** yaw substitution cannot realize HMD roll/pitch without moving the torso off the balance policy's happy path. Expect a documented, app-visible head-pose residual; prefer yaw-dominant mapping.
